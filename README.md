# FinWise — Product Experimentation & PLG Case Study

> **How might we move more users from trial to paid by helping them experience personalized financial value before asking them to subscribe?**

**Product Experimentation · Product-Led Growth · Fintech · A/B Testing · Monetization**

**Maliha Jahan**  
Product Experimentation & PLG Case study

---

## Executive Summary

FinWise is a personal finance product designed to help users understand upcoming cash-flow risks and take action before those risks become problems.

The growth challenge was not simply getting users to connect an account or use forecasting features.

The bigger question was:

**What product experience actually creates enough value for a trial user to become willing to pay?**

I approached the problem by connecting the full product journey:

**Activation → Aha Moment → Engagement → Experimentation → Monetization**

The resulting strategy places the paid boundary **after the user experiences personalized value**, rather than before it.

---

## The Product Challenge

FinWise's trial-to-paid conversion was relatively flat at approximately **2%**.

At the same time, aggregate product usage did not clearly explain why some users converted while others did not.

One important signal stood out:

> Using forecasting tools was not necessarily the same as experiencing value.

This shifted the problem from:

**“How do we get users to use more features?”**

to:

**“What is the earliest moment when the user clearly understands the value FinWise provides?”**

---

## The Aha Moment

The key value moment was defined as:

> **The user connects an account, sees a personalized 30-day cash-flow risk, and reviews one recommended action.**

Account connection and forecasting are setup activities.

The value becomes tangible when FinWise helps the user understand:

**What is likely to happen — and what should I do next?**

This Aha event became the foundation for the growth strategy, experiment design, engagement mechanic, and monetization model.

---

## The Product Bet

### Hypothesis

If FinWise shows a paid-plan prompt **after a user reviews a personalized cash-flow action**, trial-to-paid conversion will increase from:

**1.99% → at least 2.4%**

### Strategic Bet

Instead of placing the monetization boundary before users fully experience value:

> **Allow users to reach the Aha moment first, then introduce the paid relationship.**

This positions monetization as a continuation of demonstrated value rather than a barrier to discovering it.

---

## Experiment Design

**Method:** 50/50 user-level A/B test

### Control
Existing trial experience.

### Variant
Users receive the paid-plan prompt after reviewing a personalized cash-flow action.

### Primary Metric
**Trial-to-paid conversion**

Baseline:

**1.99%**

Success threshold:

**≥ 2.4%**

### Guardrail

The experiment must not materially damage product engagement.

**7-day cash-plan review rate cannot decline by more than 5% versus control.**

### Decision Discipline

Results are evaluated only after users complete the full reverse-trial period.

**No early stopping or mid-experiment success redefinition.**

---

## Engagement Mechanic

To create a meaningful reason for users to return, I designed:

### Cash Plan Freshness

The user's cash plan remains **Current** until a meaningful forecast change occurs.

When new financial information materially changes the forecast, the plan becomes:

**Needs Review**

The user reviews the updated recommendation and restores the plan to:

**Current**

The goal is not artificial gamification.

The mechanic creates a recurring behavior around a real user need:

> **Keeping financial decisions aligned with changing cash-flow conditions.**

---

## Monetization Strategy

FinWise is positioned at the **Revenue Expansion** stage of the product-led growth journey.

The recommended model is a:

### Reverse-Trial Subscription

Users can experience their first personalized forecast and recommended action during the trial.

Paid value is packaged around **Cash Plan Continuity**, including:

- Automatic forecast refreshes
- Material cash-flow alerts
- Weekly action reviews
- Additional connected accounts
- Ongoing personalized recommendations

The monetization logic follows the same value signal used throughout the product strategy:

> **Users should pay to continue receiving value they have already experienced.**

---

## Product Metrics

### Leading Indicators

- Primary account imported within 24 hours
- Personalized cash-flow action reviewed within 7 days

### Lagging Outcome

- Trial-to-paid conversion across the full reverse-trial period

### Key Insight

Historical trial-to-paid conversion remained between approximately **1.87%–2.08%**, while modeling usage showed weak correlation with conversion.

That reinforced the need to measure a more meaningful **user-level value event** rather than relying on broad feature usage.

## What I Learned

The biggest shift in my thinking was separating **product activity from product value**.

A user completing onboarding, opening a forecast, or interacting with a feature does not automatically mean the product has delivered meaningful value.

The stronger product question is:

> **What happened that made the user understand why this product matters?**

Once that value moment was defined, the rest of the strategy became more coherent:

- Onboarding drives users toward it.
- Engagement reinforces it.
- Metrics measure it.
- Experimentation validates it.
- Monetization extends it.

---

## Product Management Takeaway

This project reinforced one principle that now shapes how I approach experimentation:

> **Every product idea is a hypothesis until evidence proves otherwise.**

The PM's job is not simply to generate ideas or ship features.

It is to define the problem clearly, identify the behavior that matters, determine what evidence would change the decision, and build the fastest credible path to learning.
